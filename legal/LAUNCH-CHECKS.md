## Lawful bases, privacy risk and rights

- [ ] Record a purpose-by-purpose lawful-basis assessment. Contract is used for the requested guest/search coordination service and user-requested appeal/content hosting; consent is used for optional live GPS and personal profile-photo display. Document why core processing is necessary rather than treating everything mentioned in terms as contractual necessity. Use the [ICO lawful-basis guide](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/).
- [ ] Complete the purpose, necessity and balancing assessment for ordinary legitimate interests: security, spam/abuse prevention, moderation, support and map delivery. Consider distressed owners, location misuse, screenshots and information about non-users. The public notice's statement of a basis does not itself establish the balancing outcome. See [ICO legitimate-interests assessment guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/legitimate-interests/how-do-we-apply-legitimate-interests-in-practice/).
- [ ] Keep evidence of optional consent: who, when, how and the wording/version shown. A browser permission prompt alone does not explain every recipient or purpose. Withdrawal must be easy and does not permit silently switching the same processing to another basis. Keep archived interface wording and use the actual photo-removal/Stop controls. See [ICO consent management guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/how-should-we-obtain-record-and-manage-consent/).
- [ ] Complete DPIA screening and, where required, a DPIA before the relevant processing. Precise location, possible physical harm from disclosure, unverified participants and potentially vulnerable people need particular attention; a small user count alone is not a reason to skip assessment. [ICO DPIA criteria](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/when-do-we-need-to-do-a-dpia/).
- [ ] Establish a proportionate process for information posted about non-users and for sensitive or criminal allegations in moderation reports. Do not assume ordinary legitimate interests alone satisfies the additional rules for criminal-offence data. [ICO criminal-offence data guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/criminal-offence-data/).
- [ ] Monitor the published contact address. Record access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability and objection requests; establish identity without demanding unnecessary documents. The self-service export excludes other people's private content and staff reports about the requester, so it is **not automatically a complete subject-access response**. Review wider records and applicable redactions manually. [ICO access information](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/right-of-access/what-is-the-right-of-access/) and [response deadlines](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/right-of-access/what-should-we-consider-when-responding-to-a-request/).
- [ ] Operate a data-protection complaints process: accessible email/postal route, acknowledgement within **30 days**, appropriate investigation, progress communication and outcome without undue delay. This duty is now in force; it is separate from the usual one-month subject-access deadline. [ICO June 2026 announcement](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/) and [complaint handling guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/how-to-deal-with-data-protection-complaints/what-do-we-do-when-we-receive-a-complaint/).
- [ ] Complete the [ICO fee self-assessment](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/data-protection-fee-self-assessment/), retain the result and pay/register if required. Being an individual, operating a free service or having few users does not establish an exemption. No fee assessment or registration has been completed by publishing these pages.

## Retention and deletion operation

Agreed application lifecycle, to be checked against the final PHP implementation:

- The `sp_guest` cookie/access expires after **180 days**. It is not automatically refreshed. Cookie expiry/removal does **not** itself erase server records.
- Live GPS is excluded from responses after **30 minutes** without an update. Stop/removal/closure clears coordinates when processed. Expired coordinates are physically cleared by housekeeping; quiet sites require the maintenance job for timely cleanup.
- Closed searches become eligible **90 days after the first recorded closure**. Repeated closure and moderation must not reset this date. Legacy closed searches lacking a trustworthy closure timestamp start their retention clock at migration; do not invent an earlier date.
- Active searches become eligible **180 days after the latest approved participant write**: a contribution, location action or search/member management action. Reads, polling and pending join attempts must not prolong retention.
- Search cleanup removes related membership, messages, map reports and moderation records. Expired profiles are removed only once no retained records require their attribution. Rate counters and duplicate fingerprints have short housekeeping lifecycles.
- The API performs bounded cleanup. Configure and monitor the released daily CLI maintenance command using the same private database; a scheduled job is an operational task, not established by this document. A backlog may need multiple batches.
- Self-service deletion removes the person's profile/contributions. Deletion of every owned room also deletes other helpers' contributions and must be separately acknowledged. It is not recoverable through the public UI.

- [ ] Set and document support-email, access/error-log and backup retention with Brixly. Do not represent application deletion as immediate erasure from host backups, logs, SQLite storage remnants or other people's screenshots. Identify how deleted records will be kept out of normal use and handled after restoration.
- [ ] Test a backup restore privately, including reapplying deletions and retention. Keep database backups outside all web roots. Deployment file backups do not back up SQLite.
- [ ] Record any justified legal hold separately, limit access and duration, and explain the relevant exception to a deletion requester. The application does not implement a general evidence-hold system; do not promise one.

## Online Safety Act: assessment required

**Initial assessment, not a legal determination:** Search Pals allows people to upload appeals, photos, chat and reports that other users encounter. It therefore appears likely to be a **user-to-user service with UK links**. Its product name does not make it a statutory “search service”: the current app does not search multiple websites/databases. Private invite codes, free access, low traffic and adult terms do not by themselves establish an exemption. Confirm scope with the [Ofcom assessment tool](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/os-toolkit/assessment-tool), record the reasoning and obtain advice if an exemption is proposed.

- [ ] If in scope, name Simon as accountable owner, complete and record an illegal-content risk assessment, and choose applicable Ofcom Code measures or justified alternatives. Assess misuse such as fraud, stalking/doxxing, threats, abusive images, false appeals and offences facilitated offline. Do not simply label the service “low risk” because its intended purpose is positive. Maintain effective reporting, complaints, removal and review procedures. [Ofcom illegal-content duties](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/illegal-content-duties-under-the-online-safety-act).
- [ ] Use the **current** [Ofcom regulatory-document index](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-regulatory-documents). It lists updated user-to-user codes issued **2 November 2026**; do not rely uncritically on older 2025/early-2026 checklists or assume duties for categorised large services apply to this site.
- [ ] Complete a children's access assessment, including public appeal viewers and unregistered visitors. A statement “18+” and a self-declared age checkbox are **not highly effective age assurance** and do not establish that children cannot normally access the service. If the service is likely to be accessed by children, assess the additional children's risk/safety duties and make the resulting changes. [Ofcom children's assessment tool](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/os-toolkit/child-access-assessment/childrens-access-assessment-tool) and [assessment duties](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/childrens-access-assessment-duties-under-the-online-safety-act).
- [ ] Set a realistic operator review rota and escalation process. Room reports currently reach organisers/moderators; ensure the operator's published email route is monitored and usable when the organiser is the concern. Record decisions and reconsideration requests. Do not claim 24/7 human moderation, identity verification, AI detection or guaranteed removal times that are not provided.
